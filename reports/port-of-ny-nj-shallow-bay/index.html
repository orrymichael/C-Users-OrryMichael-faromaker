Diverse, durable trade base. Why shallow bay is insulated from the supply wave

1Port scale holds

4.4M TEUs, #1 East Coast gateway, rising trucking costs push tenants to prioritize drayage-efficient, near-port locations.

2Big-box supply overshoots

Nearly 1.0 msf of new vacant space (large-format deliveries) pushed Port Region vacancy up 110 bps to 12.5% in Q2.

3Demand for space stays real

Leasing still up 9.8% YoY and Class A rent up 7.1% — the vacancy rise is a supply story, not a demand story.

4Shallow bay can't be overbuilt

Small-bay product (<100K SF) runs ~4.8% vacant nationally — infill land scarcity caps new supply where big-box cannot.

Step 4 national small-bay vacancy benchmark: CBRE / Cushman & Wakefield industrial research, distinct from the Port Region figures above (which blend all warehouse sizes).

H1 2026 · The broader Shallow Bay thesis

The Shallow Bay thesis|Six structural drivers, not one

Port proximity is one input, not the whole case. The format's durability comes from land economics, tenancy structure and ownership fragmentation that hold regardless of any single trade cycle.

1Last-mile, not just port-adjacent

Final-mile parcel and distribution demand is driven by dense population and same-day delivery expectations — a driver independent of ocean-freight cycles.

2Land that can't be replaced

Zoning, entitlement and land cost in built-out metros make new shallow-bay supply structurally scarce. National vacancy for buildings under 100K SF: ~4.8%.

3Granular, diversified tenancy

Dozens of small, service-oriented tenants per roll — trades, light manufacturing, distribution — spread credit and re-tenanting risk versus one large user.

4Rents mark to market faster

Shorter average lease terms and below-market in-place rents let owners reprice more often than in single-tenant bulk assets.

5Fragmented ownership, our entry point

Most shallow-bay stock is owned by private, often unlevered individual owners — a durable acquisition pipeline with limited institutional competition.

6Demand from more than one source

Population growth, reshoring of light manufacturing and local service businesses all draw on the same infill space — insulating it from any single macro shock.

How goods move from the port to shallow-bay pockets

Ocean freight is only one of two inflows reaching the format — local trade and population-driven demand feed the same last-mile layer without ever touching a container.
